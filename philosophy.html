The Socratian approach

Ask, reason and discover

Inspired by the Socratic tradition, our approach uses purposeful questions to make thinking visible. The goal is not questioning for its own sake, but understanding that students can explain, test and use.

Ask

A well-chosen question reveals what a student notices, assumes and already knows. It turns uncertainty into a useful starting point.

Reason

Students compare ideas, follow evidence and explain each step. Reasoning replaces guessing with a process they can revisit and improve.

Discover

When students reach an insight through their own thinking, knowledge becomes more memorable, transferable and genuinely theirs.

Guidance with purpose

Students think for themselves—without learning alone

Thoughtful guidance gives students enough structure to move forward while preserving the productive work of noticing, explaining and making connections.

Start from what is known

New ideas become approachable when they connect to knowledge and experiences the student can already recognize.

Make thinking active

Prompts invite students to predict, compare, justify and revise instead of simply watching someone else solve the problem.

Build independent mastery

Practice strengthens both subject knowledge and the habits students need to approach unfamiliar questions with confidence.

From explanation to application

What a Learn Socratian lesson should accomplish

Every subject has its own language and methods, but the learning experience follows the same durable principles.

Notice

Identify the important information, patterns, vocabulary or relationships before deciding what to do.

Explain

Put reasoning into words. A clear explanation shows whether an idea is understood and where another question may help.

Apply

Use the idea in a new example, context or problem. Application turns an isolated answer into flexible understanding.
